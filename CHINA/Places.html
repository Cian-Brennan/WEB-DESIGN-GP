Great Wall of China

Known as the largest man-made project in the world, the Great Wall is a series of ancient fortifications that stretch approximately 21,196 kilometers. It is an integrated defense system that has defended China from attackers for millenia.

Shanghai

Being the second most populous city in the world, Shanghai is gaining more and more attention as companies and tourists alike come to the cultural center of East China. Despite it's modern glamour, you'll still find a blend of traditional, western, and oriental flair in the city.

Beijing

The capital and political, economic, and cultural center of the country. As one of the six ancient cities of China, Beijing has a long and rich history and culture that has endured to this day. It's no wonder there's more than 4 million tourists every year to explore this modern artefact.

Yangtze River

Winding about 6,380km, Yangtze River is the largest in China and the third largest in the world after the Nile in Africa and the Amazon in South America. It's central to both the development and history of China, dividing the country into North and South both geographically and culturally.

Xi'an

A city famous for being one of the birthplaces of the ancient Chinese civilization in the Yellow River Basin area 3,000 years ago. it's history of being China's ancient capital for 1,100 have endowed Xi’an with amazing historical heritages and cultural relics, which attracts people from all over the world.

Chengdu

Known for it's adorable giant pandas, Chengdu is known for it's natural abundance as the Min and Tuo rivers (two branches of the Yangtze) connect to over 40 other rivers. This gives the city over 700 square kilometres of fertile land and an abundance of mineral.

Forbidden City

The forbidden city is a sprawling palace complex with almost 1,0000 buildings spread across over 180 acres of land. It served as the imperial palace for over five centuries of emperors. Nowadays, it is also known as the 'Palace Museum' and has become an attraction for millions every year.

Jade Buddha Temple

Situated in Shanghai, the Jade Buddha Temple stands as serene escape from the bustling cityscape. The eponymous buddha's are two statues cut and decorated from blocks of whole white jade. The temple was destroyed during revolution yet the statues survived and are new placed in the new temple.

Oriental Pearl Tower

Shanghai's Oriental Pearl Tower stands as one of the tallest buildings in the world. The TV tower stood as the tallest structure in China until the completion of the nearby World Financial Center. The tower has 15 observatory levels. Lower levels contain amenities and one floor even contains a 20-room hotel.

Terracotta Army

Nestled within the “Emperor Qinshihuang's Mausoleum Site Museum” in Lintong District of Xi'an City, the Terracotta Army, also known as Terracotta Warriors and Horses, lies approximately an hour's drive from the city center. The Mausoleum of the Qin Shi Huang encompasses a vast expanse of 56 square kilometers.

Mogao Caves

Famous for its exquisite murals and its various sculptures, the Mogao Caves, also known as the Mogao Grottoes, is a group of nearly 800 caves that contain 45,000 square meters of murals and 2,415 colored sculptures. The caves are now an important historical site that traces Buddhism for 2,000 years.

Temple of Heaven

According to ancient belief, the Chinese emperor was the Son of Heaven so the Temple of Heaven was built for the ceremonies of the emperor. The temple was of the utmost importance to Chinese people as they believed even the smallest mistake in Ceremony could result in a bad year for the nation.

Li River

The 164km river is home to gorgeous Karst peaks, water buffalo patrolling the shoreline fields, China's iconic rice paddies, and school kids and fisherman that float along on bamboo rafts.
