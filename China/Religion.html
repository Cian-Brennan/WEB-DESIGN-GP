BUDDHISM

Buddhism in China has a history of nearly two thousand years, arriving along the Silk Road during the Han dynasty. Over time it blended with local beliefs, giving rise to unique traditions such as Chan Buddhism. Chinese Buddhist practices emphasise compassion, meditation, and the pursuit of enlightenment, and temples remain important cultural and spiritual centres across the country.

TAOISM

Taoism is one of China’s indigenous religions, rooted in the teachings of Laozi and the philosophy of living in harmony with the Tao (The natural order of the universe). It focuses on balance, simplicity, and inner peace. Taoist temples, rituals, and festivals remain influential in everyday Chinese culture, shaping ideas about health, nature, and spiritual energy.

CONFUCIANISM

Confucianism is not a religion in the traditional sense but a system of ethics and social philosophy based on the teachings of Confucius. It stresses the importance of education, respect for elders, social harmony, and moral behaviour. For centuries, Confucian values guided Chinese government, family life, and cultural traditions, continuing to influence society today.

ISLAM

Islam entered China through trade routes during the Tang dynasty, bringing merchants and scholars who established vibrant Muslim communities. Chinese Muslims, including the Hui and Uyghur peoples, practice a blend of Islamic faith with local cultural traditions. Mosques across China reflect this fusion, combining traditional Chinese architecture with Islamic religious design.

CHRISTIANITY

Christianity first reached China in the 7th century and later expanded through Jesuit missionaries in the Ming and Qing dynasties. Over time it developed into distinct Chinese Christian communities. Today, Christianity is practiced in both official and underground churches, contributing to China’s diverse religious landscape through charitable work, education, and community life.
