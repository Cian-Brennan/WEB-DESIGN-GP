Albanian History
-Albania is known for its unique history. There are several reasons why Albania stands out compared to other countries in Europe. Albania is one of the few countries in Europe whose people are believed to descend directly from the ancient Illyrians.
-Albania was once part of the Ottoman Empire for almost five centuries (480 years). This shaped its culture, religion, architecture and traditions in a unique way. After resisting the Ottomans for 25 years, Albania gained freedom and became a symbol of resistance across Europe.
-In 1967, Enver Hoxha turned Albania into the only country in the world to ban all religions. Churches and mosques were destroyed or closed, and practising religion was punishable. The fall of communism brought major challenges, including the collapse of pyramid schemes in 1997.

Albanian Language

The Albanian language is one of the oldest in Europe and forms its own unique branch of the Indo-European language family. It is not closely related to any other modern language, which makes it extremely rare. Albania has two main dialects: Gheg in the north and Tosk in the south, with the Tosk dialect used as the official standard.

Culture and Traditions

Albanian culture is built on the concept of “Besa,” meaning loyalty, hospitality, and keeping one’s word. Traditional dances, music using instruments like the çifteli, and clothing such as the fustanella are key parts of Albanian heritage. Hospitality is one of the most important values in Albania — guests are treated with great respect.

Important Historical Figures

Albania’s most famous national hero is Gjergj Kastrioti Skanderbeg, who resisted the Ottoman Empire for over 25 years and became a symbol of freedom across Europe. Other notable figures include Mother Teresa, one of the most respected humanitarians ever, who was born to Albanian parents.
